## Strategies

- `random`: Spends a random 30 to 65 h on random actions in random markets, including books, tests, pre-sales and invoice chasing, and answers about half of the messages with a random choice. (1.6 s for 1000 runs)
- `build_only`: Builds every feature in list order and fixes bugs. Never talks to anyone. Does the books. (0.5 s for 1000 runs)
- `sell_first`: Does outreach from week 1 without interviews, by phone in every market, then focuses on the market that asked for most demos. Builds the core feature, then the others in a random order. Does the books and chases three late invoices a week. (1.2 s for 1000 runs)
- `validate_then_build`: Interviews every market, runs one landing page test on the market its interviews favour (and drops it below 7% sign ups), builds what that market asks for, sells on its best channel, follows up with up to six open leads there a week, runs up to two pre-sale batches, and moves on after 12 weeks without a customer or two pre-sale batches without a commitment. Does the books, chases three late invoices a week, and answers the inbox by the policy in `answerInbox`. (4.1 s for 1000 runs)
- `validate_then_build_hire`: Plays like validate_then_build, and makes the one hire (a developer while wanted features are unbuilt, else a salesperson) once monthly revenue covers that salary, choosing the best impression. (4.4 s for 1000 runs)
- `validate_then_build_early_hire`: Plays like validate_then_build, and searches for the one hire as soon as its own funnel (leads per hour on the best channel, share of closed leads won, current price) says a developer or salesperson would bring in more than their salary before the last week, assuming no churn and 8 weeks from extra outreach to payment, while cash and savings hold two months of that salary. Takes the role that pays back more, and hires the best impression if it still pays back at that skill. (3.8 s for 1000 runs)
- `price_aware`: Plays like validate_then_build and raises the price by 100 EUR after every 4 trial results at a price of which at least 40% became customers. Below that it goes back one step and stays. (4.0 s for 1000 runs)
- `overworker`: Plays like validate_then_build, but plans 65 h every week. (0.2 s for 1000 runs)
- `playtest`: The hand playtest: 5 interviews per market in week 1, builds the three coworking features in weeks 2 to 4, 45 h of in person outreach to coworking in weeks 5 to 7, then every demo plus 15 h of outreach a week. (0.9 s for 1000 runs)

## Tuning notes

Tuning steps, newest last. Each one lists the change, the reason, and the effect on the targets. Details are in `docs/decisions.md`.

### Baseline (engine 0.3.0, before any tuning)

Sustainable endings: validate_then_build 92.9%, overworker 96.4%, sell_first 61.8%, playtest 41.0% (98.1% when coworking is viable), build_only 0%. The median losing run lasted 56 weeks. Targets 2, 3 and 4 failed. The full baseline report is in the commit that added the bots.

### 1. Interview signals less clean

Trap politeness 0.28 to 0.35 became 0.50 to 0.65, mediocre 0.15 to 0.30 became 0.25 to 0.40, viable 0.10 to 0.20 became 0.05 to 0.15. Interview noise 0.20 became 0.30, interviewing skill gain 0.02 became 0.01, the Urgent bucket starts at 0.90 instead of 0.80, and the feature mention chance became 0.3 + 0.3 x pain (was 0.5 x pain). Reason: in the playtest the viable market answered Urgent 5 of 5 times in week 1. Effect: after 6 interviews per market the viable one ranks first in 62% of runs (was 93%), with 44% Urgent answers (was 65%). Win rates barely moved (validate_then_build 89.7%), because bots that pick wrong still recover by moving on.

### 2. Lead volume halved

Every channel response range was halved. Reason: 45 h of in person outreach found 7 to 8 leads a week in the playtest, and section 10 of the scenario expects about one lead per 15 h early on. Effect: leads per outreach hour fell from 0.22 to 0.105 for validate_then_build (0.067 at the start, rising with selling skill). validate_then_build 78.0%, overworker 84.3%, sell_first 43.2%, playtest 16.7% (50.9% when coworking is viable, median 2 customers at week 15 there instead of 4).

### 3. Less starting cash

Company cash 15,000 became 10,000 EUR. With 12,000 EUR savings the founder has about 42 weeks of runway without revenue (was 52). Reason: losing runs lasted a median of 55 weeks, above target 4 (20 to 50), and the runway left room for many wrong turns. Effect: target 4 met (week 44). validate_then_build 73.1%, overworker 80.5%, sell_first 41.0%, playtest 15.1%.

### 4. Willingness to pay evened out across markets

Small offices 150 to 400 became 200 to 450 EUR, medical practices 100 to 250 became 180 to 320 EUR. Reason: at the default price of 300 EUR the medical market converted at about a quarter of the rate (price factor around 0.25), so validate_then_build won only 11% of runs where medical was viable against 75% where coworking was. Which market was viable decided the run more than the player did. Effect: wins by viable market for validate_then_build are now 89/95/82% (small offices/coworking/medical), and overall win rates rose (validate_then_build 88.6%). The next step brings them down for all markets alike.

### 5. Leakier funnel, viable markets slightly less in pain

Lead to demo 0.25 became 0.18 x (0.4 + 0.6 x pain), demo to trial 0.20 + 0.60 x fit became 0.05 + 0.40 x fit, selling skill gain per demo 0.01 became 0.005, viable pain 0.65 to 0.90 became 0.60 to 0.85, and churn 0.02 + 0.25 x misfit became 0.03 + 0.20 x misfit. Reason: a bot that found the viable market ended with about 19 customers, while 7 make the company profitable, so finding the market was the whole game. A version with churn 0.06 + 0.45 x misfit also met the targets but meant 20 to 30% monthly churn for a product that fits, which feels like a leaky bucket. It was rejected in favour of a slower funnel. Effect: all five targets met. validate_then_build 37.7%, overworker 55.4%, sell_first 17.5%, playtest 0.1% (it works about 20 h a week and never fixes bugs). The viable market now ranks first after 6 interviews per market in 52% of runs.

### 6. Low energy penalty and burnout (formula, from scenario section 7)

The two energy rules the scenario already specified now work: below 30 energy every sales chance is multiplied by 0.85, and three weeks in a row at 0 end the run with burnout. They were planned for Phase 4. Reason: energy had no effect, so planning 65 h every week was free. The overworker won 55.4% against 37.7% for the same play at 50 h, and the playtest showed energy climbing to 100 and staying there unused. Scenario values could not fix this, because no value made energy matter. Effect: the overworker burns out in week 7 of every run, as the plan expects. Other strategies are unchanged, because they stay at or under 50 h. Target 3 now has a 22 point margin (best strategy: validate_then_build 37.7%).

### Phase 3 review: new measurements, no tuning

Added after the review: ending week histogram, earnings of losing runs, week of the first paying customer, the `price_aware` bot, and targets 6 and 7 (not tuned toward yet). Findings at engine 0.4.0, seeds `test` (the `holdout` seeds agree within 2 points):

- Every bankruptcy falls in weeks 41 to 60, most in 41 to 50. No run dies early, so a losing run's length is set by the starting money, not by the player's choices.
- Few losing validate_then_build runs earned nothing: 9.1% never received a payment and 6.6% never had a customer. Most died with 1 to 6 customers. 11.1% reached 2,070 EUR monthly revenue at some point and still lost, from churn or by not holding it for two full months before money ran out.
- Losing playtest runs never got paid in 40.7% of cases.
- The first paying customer arrives in week 13 (median) in winning validate_then_build runs, and week 18 in losing ones. Target 7 holds, near its upper edge.
- Win rates by viable market for validate_then_build: coworking 54.4%, small offices 36.3%, medical 23.6%. The spread of 30.8 points fails target 6.
